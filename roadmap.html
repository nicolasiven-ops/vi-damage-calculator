What is planned

The workshop holds thirty-seven proposals, which is thirty-seven more than anyone can build at once. This page is the shortlist: the ones that are decided, in the order they are worth doing, each with the reason it earned a place and the work it actually needs.

Two kinds of entry live here. Features add something the app cannot do. Honesty items close a gap between what the simulation shows and what the game does — those come first when they would change a number, because a calculator that is wrong quietly is worse than one that is missing a view.

Shipped since this page was written

done

Kept rather than deleted: a roadmap that only ever grows tells you nothing about pace, and several of these were on it for a day. Each line says what it actually turned into, because the plan and the thing built are never quite the same.

  • What each item really contributes Was ITM here. The Ledger's Item value: each item taken out of the build, the same combo re-run, and the difference reported as that item's contribution — plus the same damage priced per 1,000 gold, and a line naming the one item whose removal breaks the kill. Items whose passive is not modelled yet are marked, because their number is then a floor and not an answer.
  • The combo as a timeline Workshop 01. The Gantt view: one row per step, cast blocks, hit diamonds, cooldown and attack-timer bars behind them, idle time drawn as its own span.
  • What is ready right now Workshop 02. Cooldowns are drawn on the ability icons the way the client draws them — a dark wipe that shrinks as the ability comes back, the remaining seconds on top of it, charges in the corner — and they follow the focused step, so the strip answers "what could I press at that moment".
  • The health bar from the game Workshop 03. Both sides drawn as the game draws them, green, emptying leftwards, with the focused step's damage left standing as a translucent stub and the resource rail underneath.
  • Where half the damage disappears to Workshop 04. Became the second line of every card in the Formula Inspector: the amplification, the resistance actually met and how it got there, the percent and flat reductions, each with its factor and the running total.
  • Every effect a lane Workshop 23. Buffs, shreds, crowd control and sustain each have their own lane on the timeline, grouped and coloured by what they do.
  • The formula at the hit Was: an expandable card on the timeline row. Built as the first submodule of the new Detailed View: one card per hit, with what was rolled, the measured mitigation factor, what landed, and the engine's own trace lines under it. The factor is divided out of the recorded numbers rather than recomputed, so the card cannot drift from the hit it describes.
  • Mana, spent and regained Was: the bar reads 865 / 865 for the whole combo. Costs come from Data Dragon per rank (Q 50/60/70/80/90, E 26/32/38/44/50, R 100), the pool is paid at cast and regenerates on the game's half-second clock, and an ability nobody can pay for is refused with a reason instead of quietly cast.
  • Regeneration over the combo Was: nothing regenerated while a combo ran. A tenth of the per-five-seconds figure every half second, accrued with the clock, for the target's health and the attacker's resource. Its own lane on the timeline and a "Target regenerated" chip, because it works against every damage number to its left.
  • Smite, all of it Was: base Smite only, at a number from the wrong lineage. Base corrected to 600. The three Primal Smites are in the picker with their own icons, and the pet buffs behind them are modelled: Scorchclaw's burn is 5% of the target's maximum health over four seconds and lands as real damage, Mosstomper's shield is up at the start, Gustwalker's movement speed is named as having no effect here.
  • What the caster is locked out of Was: an unverified 1.05 s that turned out not to exist. Riot's file has no such field — every leaf value between 1.00 and 1.10 is exactly 1.0 — but it has the cast time (0.25) and the airborne duration, and the wiki says Vi cannot act while she holds the target. So the ultimate now costs cast plus dash plus the 1.3 s grab, as a lock that delays acting without delaying the cooldown.
  • Basic ability haste Was: one haste number for everything. Riot scopes haste in the item text and the app now does too: Shojin's 25 and Legend: Haste's 15 shorten Q, W and E and leave the ultimate alone. The shard that looked like the culprit turned out to grant plain haste all along — checked against perks.json, where the word "Basic" appears on Legend: Haste and nowhere near it.
  • The combo ends with the target Was not on this page, and should have been. Everything after the kill was damage into a corpse: it inflated the totals, stretched the chart and diluted the damage per second. The combo now stops there, the unused steps stay in the strip greyed out, and the kill is the last line of the log — marked, because arriving at it is the point of reading down.
  • Crowd control as a window Was: part of "the windows", section below. Half of it is done as data: the engine records what the target cannot do and for how long, Vi's Q knocks up for 0.75 s and her ultimate for 1.3 s, and both draw as their own lane. What is still missing is the reading — whether a window was long enough for the target to answer.

Item passives are the one item on this list that is in progress rather than done: the foundation is in — every shop item has a verdict, the coverage test fails on an unclassified one, and the first mechanic (stacking amplifiers) is implemented and pinned by tests. 13 of 218 modelled, 11 examined and dismissed, and the rest is the work.

CMD

Typing a combo instead of clicking it

no new data medium

q1.25 aa e aa r aa is faster than six clicks and a drag, and it is a combo you can paste into a message. The strip stays — dragging is the better way to change an order — but typing is the better way to enter one, and the two edit the same list.

The part worth building carefully is the other direction: a combo that can be written can be shared. A line of text is a link, a bug report and a build guide's worth of precision, and it costs nothing to also read it back from the address bar.

What it needs. A grammar small enough to be obvious — q, q1.25 for a charge, aa, r, ignite, smite — a parser that reports what it did not understand rather than guessing, and the reverse direction that writes the current combo back out. Autocomplete is the optional half: with the engine's state available it can offer only what is actually castable at that point, and price each option in the seconds it would cost.

MIX

The best ratio of stats against this target

main feature engine is ready

This grew out of workshop 09, "where the next item is still worth it", and replaces it: a curve along one stat answers the same question one axis at a time, and the interactions are the whole point.

Not "which stat is strongest" — that question has an easy and useless answer. Which mixture is best? Against a 1,000-health carry with 30 armour, lethality is worth a great deal per point; but every point of it makes the next point of attack damage worth more, and enough attack damage makes crit worth more than either. The stats are not a ranking, they are a recipe, and the recipe changes with the target.

Why the interactions are real, and which way they run

Worth writing down, because two of the three are counter-intuitive and the feature is only trustworthy if it agrees with the arithmetic:

  • Lethality gets better as armour falls, then falls off a cliff Damage scales with 100 / (100 + armour). The derivative with respect to flat penetration is proportional to 1 / (100 + armour)², so each point removed is worth more than the one before — accelerating, not diminishing. It stops dead the moment armour reaches zero, because there is nothing left to remove: the value goes from its maximum to exactly nothing. That cliff is the single most useful thing this tool could show you.
  • Attack damage and penetration multiply each other Damage ≈ AD × 100 / (100 + armour). Your instinct was right about the direction: more penetration makes every point of AD worth more. The reason is not that lethality diminishes — it is that the two sit on opposite sides of one multiplication, so each raises the other's marginal value.
  • Crit and attack damage multiply too Expected damage ≈ AD × (1 + chance × (multiplier − 1)). A point of crit chance is worth a fixed fraction of your attack damage, so it is worth more the more of it you have — and vice versa. Crit is also worth nothing to an ability-heavy combo and everything to an attack-heavy one, which is why this has to be measured per combo and not looked up.

How it is computed: buy one gold at a time

No formula, and no search over combinations either. The engine already answers "what does this build do" in milliseconds, so the recipe is found by spending an imaginary gold budget greedily:

1. Price every stat from the cheapest single-stat component in item.json
2. Measure each one's next step +100 g of it, re-simulate, Δdamage ÷ 100 g
3. Buy the winner, repeat until the budget is gone
Out falls a mixture, and the order it was bought in

The greedy loop is the interaction model: it needs no theory about lethality and crit, because after each purchase it re-measures everything against the build it just changed. The switching points come out for free — "lethality until 18, then attack damage, then crit from 3,400 g onwards" — and those are worth more than the final mixture, because a real game passes through them in order.

How it is shown

The radar is the picture: one axis per stat, the polygon being the share of the budget the greedy loop put into each. A shape is the right form for a ratio — you recognise "penetration-heavy against a squishy" and "resistance-shredding against a tank" as shapes long before you read the numbers, and two targets side by side become two polygons.

With one honest caveat drawn on the panel rather than hidden: a radar's axes have no common unit, so the polygon must be share of gold — something that genuinely sums to one — and never "value", which would let the scaling of each axis decide the shape. Beside it: the switching path, and the damage the recommended mixture actually produces against the current mixture. That last number is what makes it a tool rather than a diagram.

The limits, stated on the panel. It optimises one combo against one target with no regard for the six-slot constraint, defensive need, or what an item bundles alongside the stat you wanted. It answers "what would pure damage against him look like", which is a sharper question than any build guide answers and still not the whole of building. Pairing it with the Ledger's Item value closes that gap: one says what the ideal mixture is, the other what the shop actually sold you.

TTK

Who is this combo actually enough against?

engine is ready many runs

One target answers one question. A grid of health against armour answers the one a build is made against: where does this combo stop killing? Every cell is a full run, coloured by whether the target dies and how fast, and the line where the colour changes is the answer — not a number to interpret but a boundary to see.

What it needs. The same simulation called once per cell, which is milliseconds each and needs no new rules — plus care that the axes are the ones that matter (health and armour for a physical combo, health and magic resist for a caster) and that the grid says which patch and which build produced it. Cheaper than it looks, and it subsumes the multi-target reading above: that is one row of this.

TGT

A target that builds itself

no new engine needs estimates, honestly labelled

A Build button next to the target's level. One click and the enemy stops being a set of empty slots: gold estimated for that level, items bought in an order somebody would actually buy them, a rune page for that champion, skill points spent the way they get spent, shards and summoners picked. Everything editable afterwards — it is a starting point, not a verdict.

This is the feature that unlocks the others. Several targets at once, the grid of who this combo is enough against and the cheapest kill all need a *reasonable* opponent, and typing six items and a rune page by hand for each of them is exactly the friction that stops anyone from using them. A default nobody has to build is worth more than a better default nobody reaches.

The chain of estimates, each one visible

Every link is either published data or an assumption, and the panel says which is which. A tool that quietly invents an opponent's gold is worse than one that asks — so the estimate is shown as a chain you can see and overrule at any link, not as a number that appears from nowhere.

Level → minutes into the game assumption, adjustable
Minutes → gold passive income + farm rate + a kill share
Gold → items an ordered core per archetype, from item.json's own recipes
Champion → runes, shards, summoners one default page per archetype
Level → skill points the ultimate at 6/11/16, the rest maxed in order
Out falls a target you would recognise from a game

What is published and what is not

Riot publishes the shop — every item, its recipe, its cost — and the champion's own numbers, which is the half that has to be exact. Riot does not publish what a jungler has by minute fourteen, and neither does anyone else in a form worth trusting. So the gold estimate is a model, it is labelled a model, and it is one slider: "roughly this far into the game". Anything derived from it inherits the label — the panel should read ≈ 8,400 g, never 8,400 g.

What it needs. A per-archetype table of build orders and rune pages — bruiser, mage, marksman, tank, assassin, enchanter — which is the only hand-maintained part and the reason this is not free; the recipe graph does the rest, since "spend 8,400 g sensibly" is a walk down an ordered list with components filling the remainder. Data Dragon's own recommended blocks are the tempting shortcut and the wrong one: they are stale enough to suggest items that no longer exist, and a default that recommends a removed item is worse than no default. Every generated choice keeps a note saying it was generated, so a target nobody edited never gets mistaken for a target somebody scouted.

01

The combo solver: what actually kills this target

main feature engine is ready

Every other tool here answers a question about a combo you already typed. This one answers the question you actually had first: what should I press? Pick a target, and the app searches orders of the abilities it has, plays each one out on the same clock the analysis uses, and reports the sequence that kills it — and how much room to spare it had.

It is a search, not a formula, which is exactly why it is possible. The simulation already refuses to cast an ability that is on cooldown, already knows a charged Q takes 1.25 s to hold and that E carries its own attack, already applies the armour shred after the hit that caused it. A solver does not need any new rules — it needs to call simulate a few thousand times and sort the results.

Search space 4 castables + attacks + 2 summoners, length ≤ 8
Pruned by cooldowns, mana, unlearned ranks, duplicate prefixes
Scored by time to kill, then damage to spare
Answer Q(1.25) → AA → E → AA → R → AA · dead at 3.26 s

What it needs. A breadth-first walk over prefixes with the state the engine already carries, so a branch that cannot reach the kill in the remaining window is dropped whole rather than played out. Two scores, not one: fastest kill and least overkill answer different questions, and the second is what tells you which ability you did not need. Then the part that makes it a main feature rather than a curiosity — it has to explain itself, so every result links back into the timeline that produced it.

The honest limits. It can only find what the engine can simulate: no dodging, no walking out of range, no second champion answering back. It searches presses, not fights. Said plainly on the panel, that is a feature — it is the theoretical maximum, and the gap between it and what you manage is the thing worth practising.

02

What a stat is worth against what it costs

main feature data available

Measure what +10 of each stat does to this combo, divide by what the shop charges for it, and the ranking that comes out is not a build guide's opinion — it is your build's own arithmetic. The shop prices stats at a fixed rate; your combo does not value them at that rate; the gap is where building decisions live.

The prices need no table: Riot sets them through the cheapest single-stat component, and those are in the patch data already downloaded. Long Sword at 350 g for 10 AD makes AD 35 g a point, on this patch, and the number moves when Riot moves it.

Worked out in full in the workshop, including where it goes next — the ranked next item, damage per slot rather than per gold, the curve instead of the single derivative, and the two-axis comparison of shop-efficient against combo-efficient. Read it there.

03

Against more than one target at a time

shape still open engine is ready

One target answers "does this kill him". A row of targets answers the question a build is actually made against: who does this combo beat and who does it not? The same combo against a 1,800 HP / 30 armour mid-laner, a 2,400 / 90 fighter and a 3,600 / 200 tank is three different builds' worth of information, and the engine can produce all three in the time it takes to redraw the panel.

The form is deliberately left open — a strip of verdict tiles, a small matrix, or the target column turning into a list of pinned targets. The useful version is the one that makes the threshold visible: not three numbers, but where along the armour axis this combo stops killing.

04

The crit spread: a range, not an average

data available small

With any crit chance at all, one number is a lie of omission. "550 damage" is the expected value; the combo actually deals somewhere between the no-crit floor and the all-crit ceiling, and whether the floor kills is the only version of the question that matters when you are committing to a dive.

So: whenever crit chance is above zero, the total gets a bar instead of a figure — floor, expected, ceiling, with the target's health marked on the same scale. Where that line falls inside the bar is the answer: left of the floor is a guaranteed kill, right of the ceiling is impossible, and anywhere between is a probability that can be stated.

What it needs. Two extra runs with critMode forced to never and always, which the engine already supports and the UI already exposes as a toggle. The probability of the kill is the harder half and the more valuable one: with n critable hits it is a sum over which of them crit, and it is worth doing properly rather than as a normal-curve approximation.

05

Item passives, all of them

honesty item the biggest number gap

Fifteen item passives are modelled. There are hundreds of items, and every unmodelled one is a build whose damage the app under-reports — not by a rounding error but by whole abilities' worth. A Sundered Sky that does not crit its first hit, a Black Cleaver that does not stack, an Eclipse that never fires: the stat line is counted in full, and the part that made you buy the item contributes nothing.

The app is at least honest about it — each such item is badged in the panel and listed in the notes as "stats count in full, the passive is not modelled". That is the right behaviour and no substitute for the number. This is the largest single gap between what the calculator says and what the game does, which is why it sits above every view on this page.

  • On-hit and on-cast riders Extra damage folded into an attack or triggered by a cast. The hooks exist and are used by the spellblade family, Nashor's, Wit's End and Titanic Hydra. This is the cheapest category to extend: one entry per item, and the engine already calls it.
  • Conditional first hits Sundered Sky's guaranteed crit, Eclipse's two-hit window, Voltaic's energised proc. Three are in; the pattern is a counter plus an internal cooldown, which the runtime already supports. Mostly a matter of reading each tooltip carefully and writing the test that pins it.
  • Stacking amplifiers Spear of Shojin's Focused Will, anything that grows while you keep dealing damage. Needs one thing the interface does not have: the amplify hook is told the damage type but not whether it came from an ability, a passive or an attack. Shojin only amplifies the first two, so this category waits on that signature.
  • Actives Everything with a button: Youmuu's, Hydra's, Randuin's on the target's side. A combo can already hold an item step; nothing resolves it. Needs the step to name which item, and the item to declare a cast time and a cooldown — the same shape abilities already use.
  • Health and resistance scaling Passives that read the target's maximum health, or your own. BotRK and Titanic are in and prove the shape works; the rest are the same lookup against numbers the simulation already has.
  • The target's items Randuin's damage reduction, Bramble's grievous wounds, armour from the shield. The target's gear currently contributes stats only, by design — but a Randuin's that soaks nothing makes the tank matchup read better than it is. This one belongs with the duel work.

The order that pays. Not alphabetically and not by popularity — by how wrong the app currently is about a build someone would actually bring. For Vi that means the mythic-tier bruiser items first, then the lethality line, then the on-hit line. Every entry arrives with a test that fixes its number, because an item passive modelled from memory is worse than one that is honestly absent.

06

One panel for items, runes and summoners

layout no new data

Three panels stacked down the sidebar is three panels' worth of height for three things you never look at simultaneously. One panel with a switch at the top — items, runes, summoners — costs the height of the tallest of them once, and the sidebar stops being a scroll.

The item half then gets the room it needs. It is currently a strip of six slots and a picker that opens under them: narrow, one column, and a search box doing the work a grid should do. Instead: a fixed-height grid showing every item at once, filtered by class, and you drag the one you want onto the slot you want it in. The six slots stay where they are; what changes is that choosing stops being a search and becomes a look.

What it needs. The switch is the shape the analysis column already uses for its three views, so it exists. Drag-and-drop exists too — the combo strip is built on dnd-kit — so an item tile is a draggable and a slot is a drop target, which also gives dragging between two slots for free. The fixed height is the part to get right: it has to hold the grid at the widths the sidebar actually gets, which means the tiles size to the column rather than the column to the tiles.

08

More than one champion

large architecture

Everything here is built for Vi and almost nothing in it is about Vi. The engine takes a champion module: an ability list, a runtime with hooks for casting, attacking and hitting, and a table of values that prefers Riot's own formulas over hand-maintained ones. A second champion is a second module, and the first one to be written will find every place the interface quietly assumed there was only ever one.

The target side is the other half. A champion target already resolves real base stats, growth, items and runes — but nothing it does is simulated, which is the difference between a calculator and a duel. That is the point at which 1 versus 1 becomes possible, and the reason the two sidebars were built from one mould.

The honest order. Second champion first, because it is the change that proves the module interface. Then the target acting, because it needs incoming damage, which needs mitigation on your side, which needs the defensive half of the stat model the sheet already shows.

09

The windows: what the target can do while this happens

for the duel timeline is ready

The timeline already knows every stretch of time a combo spends: charging, dashing, locked in an animation, waiting on a cooldown — and now also what the target cannot do, since crowd control is recorded as a window with its own lane. Turn that around and it answers a question about the other side — when could they have reacted, and to what? A 1.25-second charge is 1.25 seconds of warning. A knock-up is a stretch in which they could not answer at all. The gaps between are the windows a Flash or a Cleanse fits into.

On its own that is a good reading of a combo. Under a duel it becomes the spine of the thing: two champions on one clock, and every window on one side is a decision point on the other. That is why it sits here rather than in the workshop — it is the piece the 1v1 needs before the 1v1 can mean anything.

The proposal and its mock-up are in the workshop as section 35. What it needs beyond that: the spans marked by whether the target is able to act during them, which means the engine has to record crowd control it applies as a state and not only as a bar — and the same for what the caster is locked out of, below.

✱

Known gaps

reference

Not planned work — the standing list of what the app does not model, so no number here is mistaken for a complete one. The app says the same thing in place: every unmodelled rune, item passive and summoner spell is listed in the notes panel at the bottom of its own sidebar.

  • Nothing hits back No incoming damage, no mitigation on your side, no death. Consequence: Barrier, Heal, lifesteal and shields are stats without a situation. Vi's Blast Shield is simulated as a number that is granted and never spent.
  • Crowd control is recorded, not resolved Knock-ups and slows are windows on the timeline; tenacity is summed and consumed by nothing. Nothing acts against the windows yet, because the target does not act at all. That arrives with the duel.
  • No map No range checks, no walking, no dodging. Flash, Ghost and Teleport are therefore picks with no effect, and the notes panel says so for each of them.
  • Item actives Passives that fire on hit or on cast are modelled; actives are not. The combo can hold an item step, but nothing resolves it yet.
  • Most runes Keystones that deal damage are modelled; the rest contribute stats. Each unmodelled pick is badged in the rune panel and listed in the notes, so the gap is visible where the choice is made.
  • Stacking passives that need a fight Spear of Shojin's Focused Will, Conqueror, anything counting takedowns. They need a duration of combat the simulation does not have. They will arrive as stated assumptions, clearly marked, or not at all.
